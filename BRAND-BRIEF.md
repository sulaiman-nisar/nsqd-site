## 1. The one-paragraph elevator

NSQD is a bespoke fabrication and brand activation studio operating from Jebel Ali in Dubai and Wynberg in Cape Town. Two cities, one team, eight machines. End-to-end production for clients who arrive with a brief; by-the-part manufacturing for clients who arrive with a shopping list.

## 5. Brand voice

### What it sounds like

- **Hip-Hop Lo-fi Funk.** Workshop with a sunflower-yellow streak, not a brand consultancy.
- **Confident, not corporate.** Statements, not slogans. Facts, not adjectives.
- **Show the craft.** Name materials. Name machines. Name kerf widths. Name deadlines. Name brands (with permission).
- **Tight cadence.** Short sentences. Periods. Line breaks where a comma might tempt.
- **Honest.** No "world-class," no "passionate about excellence."

### What it never sounds like

- **No agency-speak.** Banned words and phrases:
  - synergy, unlock, leverage, drive, scale (as a verb), pivot
  - best-in-class, world-class, cutting-edge
  - passionate about, dedicated to, committed to
  - excellence, innovation, transformation, journey
  - "we exist to..." / "we believe in..." / "we are on a mission to..."
- **No motivational quote graphics.** "Monday motivation," "build something," etc.
- **No engagement bait.** "Drop a 🔥 if...", "DOUBLE TAP if you...", "Comment YES if..."
- **No hashtag spam.** 20+ hashtag stacks at the bottom of a post are off-brand.
- **No emoji storm.** Sparingly OK (1-2 max per post). Never as substitutes for words.
- **No editorial polish for its own sake.** Pentagram aesthetics, gradient meshes, serif display, "creative agency" tone are explicitly out.
- **No em-dashes (—).** They read as an AI tell. Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop instead. This applies to every caption, headline, and line of copy. (En-dashes in number ranges like "2404–2409" are fine.)

### On-brand examples

> Two weeks. CO2 laser, UV flatbed, bench-tested bearings. Spin wheel for a fitness pop-up. Built, branded, on-site.

> Pantone 2407 C, matched leaf-by-leaf against printed fabric. Bamboo ribs CNC-cut, slow feed, clean edge. Wood-burned handle, one at a time. Fan kit out the door.

> Dubai workshop, 7am. Three palm fronds on the bench, two more in the spray booth. Eid Al Etihad delivery this week.

> Every panel taken in and out before paint. No surprises at install.

### Off-brand examples (do not write like this)

> "At NSQD, we leverage cutting-edge fabrication to unlock brand experiences that move the needle. World-class craftsmanship is in our DNA. #passion #innovation"

> "Big things happening at NSQD this week. Stay tuned. 🚀✨💪"

> "Monday motivation: success is built one part at a time. What are you building today?"

> "DOUBLE TAP if you've ever seen a 14-foot palm frond up close. 🌴🌴🌴"

## 6. Visual identity

| Token | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Brand color | **#FFB600** (saffron amber) | Used as a "highlighter" accent. Never as a button. WhatsApp green is the action color. |
| Background | **#0A0A0A** (ink) | Default site bg. |
| Text | **#FFFFFF** (paper) | Headlines / primary text. |
| Mute | rgba(255,255,255,0.62) | Secondary text. |
| Edge | rgba(255,255,255,0.10) | Hairline borders. |
| Sans (body) | Poppins | 400/500/600/700/800 |
| Spec (UI / labels) | IBM Plex Sans | 400/500/600/700 |
| Mono | IBM Plex Mono | Used for callouts and stats. |

### Aesthetic guidelines

- **Industrial workshop**, not "creative agency."
- **Lo-fi grain over polish.** Site uses a 4.5% film grain overlay sitewide.
- **Sharp typography**, no serifs in display.
- **Saffron yellow only as accent / highlighter** — never as a fill or button.
- **Photos:** real workshop, real materials, real machines. Workshop clutter is allowed (kerf scraps, paint splatter, the Pantone book on the bench).
- **Avoid:** pastel palettes, glassmorphism, gradient meshes, generic "tech startup" looks, animated cursors, marquees, pink palettes, licensed IP imagery.

## 8. Content categories that work

1. **Process shots** — fabrication mid-flight (laser head cutting, UV carriage passing, 3D-print bed mid-build, paint table)
2. **Detail close-ups** — kerf edges, finished surfaces, hardware, engraving
3. **Workshop honesty** — bench shots with raw materials, machines, scrap, Pantone books
4. **Named delivery moments** — finished pieces on-site, on a broadcast, in a venue (with client permission)
5. **Build-to-install timelines** — countdown posts ("crating tomorrow," "doors open Friday")
6. **Capability deep dives** — one machine, what it does, what we make with it
7. **City-specific posts** — Dubai workshop OR Cape Town studio
8. **The Floor** — the homepage pipeline (vector → laser → UV → 3D → assembly) can be referenced and broken down per stage

### Content to avoid

- Generic motivational posts
- Hashtag-heavy lifestyle content
- Quote graphics with no context
- "Behind the curtain" reveals that aren't real workshop
- Anything that reads like an agency brag-sheet
- Posts about how busy we are (clients hate this)
- "Hiring soon!" announcements that aren't tied to a specific role

## 15. Things the AI should ask before posting

If the context is unclear, the AI should pause and ask:

1. **"Is this client cleared to be named?"** — default to no if not certain
2. **"Is this image one we own / have rights to publish?"** — workshop shots = ours; client-provided broadcast stills need a credit line
3. **"Are we naming specific numbers (deadlines, dimensions, quantities)?"** — names must be accurate. If unsure, soften to "weeks" or "at scale" rather than invent.
4. **"Is the caption longer than 5 sentences on Instagram, or 12 on LinkedIn?"** — if so, tighten.
